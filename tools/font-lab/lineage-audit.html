What the 41% actually means

The headline number that looks like a failure, and isn't

Read cold, 41% agreement with an outside source sounds like the index is wrong more often than right. Reading the nineteen disagreements individually tells a different story, because several of them are the other source being wrong.

Its suggested free stand-in for Whitney — a wayfinding gothic — is Hind, a Devanagari-first family. For Frutiger, also Hind. For Akzidenz Grotesk, the reference 19th-century grotesk, it proposes Archivo Black, which is a poster weight. For Clarendon, a text Egyptian, it proposes Ultra, a fat face. Those are not near misses; they are category errors, and our index disagreeing with them is the index working.

The disagreements that survive scrutiny all say the same thing: some faces genuinely belong to two lineages, and the data model can only hold one.

Proxima Nova is filed under geometric sans; the external source pairs it with Roboto, an American grotesk. Both are defensible — the family's own description in our index is "geometric skeleton with grotesk proportions." Graphik, Circular and Founders Grotesk produce the same argument. A single cluster field forces a choice the type doesn't make.

That is the most valuable finding in this audit, and it is a schema change rather than a data correction: the paid index and the 663-family inventory both need a secondary lineage. Search would then resolve Proxima Nova into both S03 and S04 instead of picking a side, which is closer to how anyone actually uses it.

The embedding test

fontjoy's 200-dimension vectors over 1,883 Google font variants

This is the only objective similarity signal in anything I found — a learned representation, not a designer's opinion, which makes it a real check rather than a second opinion. Coverage is the catch: the dataset is from an earlier era of Google Fonts and reaches only 57 of your 145 families. Anybody, Big Shoulders, Boldonse, the Protest family, Tektur and Tilt Warp all postdate it.

Within those 57, the taxonomy separates cleanly. Same-lineage pairs average a cosine similarity of 0.53 against 0.28 for cross-lineage pairs — a gap of 0.25 on a scale where anything above about 0.1 is meaningful with this many pairs.

Nearest-neighbour agreement reads as 17.5%, which looks poor until you notice the base rate. Those 57 families spread across 27 lineages, and 13 of them are the only member of their lineage in the subset — their nearest neighbour cannot be same-lineage, so they can never agree. A random guess lands in the right lineage 3.8% of the time. Restricted to the 44 families that could agree, the figure is 23%.

Two seams, then, and one policy. The R06/R07 slab boundary and the S07/S09 condensed-industrial boundary each get crossed by more than one pair, which points at the definitions rather than at individual rows. The script-routing rule is doing exactly what the research document says it should, but the tool should say so where it shows a match.

What to change

Ordered by how much it moves the tool

Add a secondary lineage

A schema change to the paid index and the family inventory. Proxima Nova, Graphik, Circular and Founders Grotesk all sit legitimately between the geometric and American-grotesk lineages; forcing one loses information and makes search miss. This is the change that most improves the answers.

Move Khand, and look again at Roboto Condensed

Khand from S09 to S07 is supported by two independent nearest-neighbour results. Roboto Condensed is the same argument one step weaker.

Restate the two porous boundaries

R06 against R07, and S07 against S09. Each was crossed by multiple pairs, which reads as a definition that needs sharpening rather than rows that need moving.

Surface the script-routing rule in the tool

When a match is separated by script rather than by shape, Font Lab 3 should say so. Otherwise the two most similar faces in the whole set sit in different lineages with no explanation on screen.

Re-run the embedding test as coverage improves

88 of your 145 families are too recent for this dataset. The same test on a current embedding would be a far stronger check, and the script to run it is already written.
